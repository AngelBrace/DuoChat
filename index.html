DuoChat 2.0

DuoChat 2.0 reads Twitch and YouTube live chat together, on a Windows window or on an Android phone or tablet. You still type in your chat client. This is an interactive mock up of the app with two fake logs. When online, real Twitch channel emotes are shown. Click or tap through it the same way you use the app. 'Browser', 'ForgetMeNot', the Highlights views, Twitch IRC, and windows that float on their own are Windows. This copy matches the app.

Past Twitch chat is Windows. Paste a VOD, highlight, or clip link. Leave the times blank for the whole chat, or set a start and an end. Fetch opens that chat in LogViewer and saves it beside the daily logs. It is not added to today's live chat. Cancel stops the walk.

Tabs

While DuoChat opens today's log and the saved rooms, DuoChat.png sits in the middle of the window. That splash goes away when startup is ready. The tab buttons sit in one row in front of the DuoChat 2.0 title. Browser, Highlights, ScratchPad, and ForgetMeNot show their pictures on the tab. Twitch IRC Chat shows the purple IRC picture, then Chat. When more tabs open, the row wraps onto a second line. 'Browser' is first on Windows and shows the stream. Its bookmarks include Chat Bubbles and Word Bubbles, which open ChatBubbles.html and WordBubbles.html beside DuoChat.exe and pack one saved log when you choose a file. 'Twitch Chat' and 'YouTube Chat' are the live chat rooms. They show the chat only and stay open while you look at the log. 'All' mixes both rooms by the clock. On All, Twitch, and YouTube, a Twitch line starts with the Twitch picture and a YouTube line starts with the YouTube picture. Twitch Chat, YouTube Chat, and Twitch IRC Chat start with the time. 'Twitch' and 'YouTube' each keep one room. 'Highlights' keeps the lines you might have missed. 'ForgetMeNot' keeps track of who you greeted. 'ScratchPad' is the last tab. Inside it, 'No Room' drafts a message, 'DuoPaint' is the drawing sheet, 'Copied' keeps the lines you copied, and 'Notes' is last. On Android, 'Chat Bubbles' draws 100 messages at a time as circles, 'Word Bubbles' packs the top words from 40 up to 1000, and 'Setup' can show an 'Error Logs' tab just before 'Copied'. Drag or swipe a screen left or right to pull the next one over. The row ends at the first and last tab. The practice buttons below still include the Android Chat Bubbles and Word Bubbles screens.

The top bar

Last chat takes a nickname. Press Enter to see that person's last message. The log starts a new file at midnight, and the row numbers count from the first line that day. Clear counts the rows again. Every message row keeps its number at the far right end of the row, apart from the words.

Highlights

Pick one view at a time. Highlights lists lines with your highlight words, such as taters. Channel lists lines from or about the open Twitch channel. Me lists lines that mention or reply to your Me name from 'Setup', including a highlight word that starts that name. A line goes to one button, and Me comes before Channel. Opening a button shows the lines already waiting there. Here Me is Greenleaf_Legolas007, so Mithrandir’s timeout shows up. System lists subs, gifts, and redeems. StreamElements lists those lines. Gifts/Subs lists the people who subbed, gifted, or raided, oldest first. A waiting name stays orange until you click it, and that button is marked Replied so it is not sent again. When a new line or gift arrives, the Highlights button stays orange until you click each one. Clicking Highlights opens the button that line came from. When more than one is waiting on that button, the button shows the count. Clicking a line removes it. Clear removes every item on the open button, including Gifts/Subs. Notes now live at the end of ScratchPad. Hold the mouse on a message time to see the full date. Drag the Highlights tab down to open it in its own window, and click X on that panel to dock it back.

ForgetMeNot

Fill chats, when on, also puts that line in the Twitch Chat box and the Twitch IRC Chat box. Enter sends it there. A parted name stays parted when they speak or when you say hello, and you move them back to chatters with the mouse. Parted names outline their chat lines and paint their Twitch IRC rows magenta. A chatter line is outlined in that nickname's color. An Arrival line keeps a green wash and italic text until you say hello. Nick tint, when on, also paints a light tint of the nickname color behind the row. Text shadow, when on, draws one dark copy of the nickname and the message, one pixel down and to the left, with the letter in front. The clock, emotes, and orange chips stay plain. White and Pink use dark gray. Twitch's web page is unchanged. A bot line is skipped. Unknowns are people who may be banned. Dropping a name there takes them off the greeting count, and a chatter or parted name drops that count by one. Your own hello or goodbye lines in chat save those names too. Right-click a button to add @name to the line. The list is saved, so a restart brings the buttons back. On an offline day a name stays green until you greet them, then stays blue. Each name has one color. Chatters and Parted clear 15 minutes after a bot, StreamElements, or System announces that the stream is over. Arrivals stay green until you say hello, including after a restart and a new day's log. Unknowns stay. The log file starts at midnight, and the buttons stay through midnight.

Windows of their own

On Windows, drag a tab button down to open that screen in its own window. DuoChat remembers its size and place. Floating windows snap to the screen edges and to each other, and windows that touch resize together. Click X on that panel to close the window and dock it back in DuoChat.

Word Bubbles

On Windows, the Word Bubbles bookmark opens WordBubbles.html beside DuoChat.exe. Choose a saved log and it packs that file once. The common-word list is inside the page. The wheel zooms and the middle mouse drags. On Android, opening 'Word Bubbles' takes a picture of the chat so far. How many starts at 40 and goes up to 1000. Change All, Twitch, YouTube, How many, or Common words, and it takes another picture. Long-press taters to pin “what is taters?!”. Tap the note. A smaller note appears on tater, and on the slow-mode line, because that line contains tater. A message already on screen is not added again. × on a branch closes that branch. × on the first note, another long-press, or Back, closes the tree. Pinch to zoom and use two fingers to drag. Zoomed in, circles outside the window are skipped.

Saved logs

'Setup' starts with Save daily logs checked. Open the practice day. The date stays, and the log size in kilobytes sits beside it. A large file is a Twitch session. A tiny file is a day without one. The tab reads 'LogViewer' and the date, so it is obvious those rows are a saved file and not the live rooms. Opening the day, or fetching a past Twitch chat, shows a progress card while the file is read and the lines are drawn. On Windows, that page is Lines and Notes. All, Twitch, and YouTube pick which room those lines and notes show. × closes the tab. On Android, All, Twitch, and YouTube also pick the room for Chat Bubbles, and Lines, Chat Bubbles, and Word Bubbles pack that snapshot. × closes those views too. Today's Android 'Chat Bubbles' and 'Word Bubbles' stay on the live rooms. On Windows, Open folder shows Documents\DuoChat\Logs. On Android, Open folder hands that day’s plain text to another app. A VOD, highlight, or clip link in 'Setup' also opens in LogViewer. Install a new Android copy over the app already on the phone. Logs, the scratch pad, and the saved rooms stay. Uninstalling the app removes that folder. If a chat tab stays blank, turn on 'Error Logs' in 'Setup'. The tab sits just before 'Copied'. Open it and take a screenshot.

Message rows

In 'Setup', Text sets the message size. It starts at 14, and the time stays a little smaller. Readability grows the whole window in steps of 25% from 100% to 200%. Up makes a row taller. Down makes it shorter. The sample changes as you choose, and every message row follows. The choice is remembered.

Browser

'Browser' is the first tab on Windows. It opens the default Twitch channel, and Home goes back there. Bookmarks starts with Google, Twitch, YouTube, Discord, Slither, Moonlander, Pac-Man, Torus, and DuoChat. Bookmark this page adds the open page, and Remove takes one off the list. History lists recent pages and pages by day. Extensions takes an unpacked extension folder. Drag the tab down to open it in its own window.

Twitch IRC

'Twitch IRC Chat' is Windows. It shows the checked channel in the Twitch look, or with IRC marks when Name Style is IRC marks. When 'Twitch Chat' is open on the same channel, a matching line takes that window's name color and badge pictures. The bar has Chat and IRCMonitor. Chat is the readable list. IRCMonitor is the raw log inside this tab, and it opens scrolled to the newest line on the connection that is already open. A repeated emote stays on that one line, and a shorter copy is removed before the window opens. The gear sets font size, edit box size, line spacing, name style, readable colours, timestamps, replies, show nick list, nick badges, and show /me. Show nick list puts one name column beside whichever view is open. Nick badges shows badge pictures, and off keeps the ordinary marks. Show /me hides that button. The ✦ popup stays open so a custom button can be clicked as many times as you want, and Close puts it away. A custom button GIF plays while that popup is open. Shift+right-click removes one. Hover an ASCII name and the face pops out at the text size of the chat you are in. Edit ASCII opens the text file so you can add your own. Web chat loads first, the last 50 messages or the last hour, whichever Twitch shows. IRC stays optional and starts after that page. The lines IRC does not already have are written once from that web chat, with the text, badges, colours, emotes, and reply. After that, IRC logs on its own. A magenta name paints that row magenta. A green name outlines it in green.
